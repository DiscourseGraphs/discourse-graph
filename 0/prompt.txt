  How Hamming presents Kraft:
  - He calls the Kraft sum **K** = Σ 1/2^{l_i} ≤ 1 (base 2), and says it means "there cannot be too many short symbols". The equations are images in the PDF. The K notation shows in the text: "K' and K'' for their sums" and "If K were > 1".
  - **Kraft proof:** induction on the decoding tree. Joining two subtrees adds one to every length, so K = ½(K' + K'').
  - **McMillan:** covered in Ch. 10. He raises K to the n-th power, counts N_k ≤ 2^k, and argues that K^n would outgrow a linear bound in n. His conclusion is that instantaneous decodability "costs us nothing", so he uses prefix codes from then on.
  - **Worked examples:** lengths 1,3,3,3 and 2,2,3,3,4,4,4,4. He also notes that meeting the Kraft inequality means a code *exists*; it does not prove a given code is decodable.
  - **Useful for the cross-entropy and KL module:** in Ch. 13 he builds pseudoprobabilities Q_i = 2^{-l_i}/K and applies Gibbs' inequality to get H ≤ L (Shannon's noiseless coding theorem). That is the cross-entropy argument without the name.
