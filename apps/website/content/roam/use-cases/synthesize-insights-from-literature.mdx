## Literature synthesis with discourse graphs

Discourse graphs turn literature synthesis into a **question-directed activity**: instead of merely absorbing disconnected insights from different sources, the discourse graph workflow orients your reading around a central guiding question of your own devising.

- <NodeTag type="hyp" />
  This purpose-driven reading increases engagement with the text and retention
  of salient data.
- <NodeTag type="hyp" />
  It also incentivizes reading widely and strategically, as papers from many
  different authors/fields may have made small but significant contributions to
  your question.

There's a bit of a chicken-and-egg problem here, as engaging in question-driven analysis of the literature first requires the development of a question, which usually involves at least some loosely-directed exploration. Discourse graphs accommodate both phases of the explore/exploit curve.

### Managing literature sources with Zotero

<Callout type="info" emoji="💡">
  For more detailed instructions on importing and citing articles from Zotero,
  check out the tutorials in the [template lab
  graph](https://roamresearch.com/#/app/template-lab/page/9we_HJeEH)
</Callout>

Here's how to get started:

1. Install and enable the ZoteroRoam extension from Roam Depot.
2. Install the [Better BibTeX plugin](https://retorque.re/zotero-better-bibtex/installation/) for Zotero and open Zotero Settings.

   <Image
     src="/docs/roam/zoteroroam01.png"
     alt="Better BibTeX settings in Zotero"
     width={794}
     height={403}
     className="docs-bordered-image"
   />
   _Installing Better BibTeX_

3. Set your **Citation key format** to: `auth.lower + year + veryshorttitle(1,0).lower`
4. Set the **autoPinDelay** parameter to 1.
5. Pin your existing **citekeys**: select all items in view (either with the mouse or the `Ctrl-A` keyboard shortcut), right-click, then navigate to **Better BibTeX > Pin BibTeX key**.

   <Image
     src="/docs/roam/zoteroroam02.png"
     alt="Pinning BibTeX keys in Zotero"
     width={1054}
     height={1039}
     className="docs-bordered-image"
   />
   _Pinning citekeys_

6. Back in Roam, click the ZoteroRoam icon at the top right of your screen.

   <Image
     src="/docs/roam/zoteroroam03.png"
     alt="The ZoteroRoam icon in the Roam topbar"
     width={492}
     height={229}
     className="docs-bordered-image"
   />
   _The ZoteroRoam icon_

7. You'll be prompted to enter an API key and a nickname for your personal or group library.

   <Image
     src="/docs/roam/zoteroroam04.png"
     alt="ZoteroRoam API key prompt"
     width={597}
     height={291}
     className="docs-bordered-image"
   />
   _API key prompt_

8. Log in to Zotero on the web and navigate [here](https://www.zotero.org/settings/security#applications) to get your personal API key. Click `Create new private key` to generate an API key and enter it at the prompt (and record it elsewhere for later use). Your User ID for API calls goes in the area marked with the orange box.

   <Image
     src="/docs/roam/zoteroroam05.png"
     alt="Create new private key button on zotero.org"
     width={308}
     height={98}
     className="docs-bordered-image"
   />
   _Creating an API key_

   <Image
     src="/docs/roam/zoteroroam06.png"
     alt="ZoteroRoam prompt with the User ID field highlighted"
     width={597}
     height={291}
     className="docs-bordered-image"
   />
   _User ID field highlighted in orange_

9. Now set your permissions. You will probably want to give ZoteroRoam notes access and write access if you want to import your notes and add items to Zotero from Roam.

## Organizing your reading

At the outset of the _exploration_ phase, you probably have a topic in mind, or an assertion vaguely in the shape of a question/hypothesis that needs further refinement. You may be reading the literature to understand prior art on the topic. At this point, before a Question or Project has coalesced, some users like to take notes on their **Daily Notes Page**.

### EVD and CLM mining

As you read the literature, capture claims and evidence relevant to your Question. You can track them on the Question page itself, on the relevant Source page, or anywhere else in your graph -- as long as you tag relevant items with `[[[[QUE]] - {your question}]]`, these mentions will be linked at the bottom of your Question page.

## Integrating findings from the literature with your research

The [discourse canvas](/docs/roam/guides/using-the-canvas) can be used to assemble your claims and evidence into an overview of the state of the field in relation to your target question.

<Image
  src="/docs/roam/merton-canvas-001.png"
  alt="Literature synthesis on the discourse canvas"
  width={1618}
  height={1010}
  className="docs-bordered-image"
/>
_Literature synthesis on the discourse canvas_

As you collect and assimilate claims and evidence sourced directly from the literature, you will probably develop a few claims of your own that derive from your reading but aren't directly stated in any of the articles. This is your **Synthesis Claim**: an initial position on the question that you'd like to test further.

<Image
  src="/docs/roam/merton-canvas-002.png"
  alt="A synthesis claim highlighted on the canvas"
  width={1614}
  height={998}
  className="docs-bordered-image"
/>
_Developing a synthesis claim (highlighted)_

This synthesis claim can be used as a springboard for more directed reading and the development of an experiment, simulation, or other test.

Once you've metabolized the claim to find testable components, you can add those components to your graph as **Hypotheses** and begin developing **Experiments**.

The multiple layers of the tldraw discourse canvas facilitate organizing your research campaign into _literature review_ and _project planning_ phases.

<Image
  src="/docs/roam/merton-canvas-003.png"
  alt="Planning phase on the canvas"
  width={1009}
  height={950}
  className="docs-bordered-image"
/>
_A claim becomes a hypothesis, and the inspiration for two potential
experiments_

In the discourse graph workflow, these phases are largely simultaneous and mutually informative.
